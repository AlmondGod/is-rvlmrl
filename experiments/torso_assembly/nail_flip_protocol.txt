Scope: finite-strength, load-bearing simulated fasteners and a two-gripper flip test.
No physical hardware validation. No glue in this experiment.

Insertion model: instantaneous, ray-gated deposition at a commanded gun visit.
Requires the actual simulated ray to encounter the two intended materials,
stand-off <=12 mm, gap <=6 mm, receiver embed >=15 mm, and no duplicate shot.
Actual material state is read ONLY by the physics engine, just as contact
solvers read it; no connection anchors or latent strength enter policy inputs.
This is NOT resolved nail impact, driving resistance, crushing or a contact-triggered
industrial nailer. The event should not be described as validated nail driving.

Connection model: isotropic translational spring-damper at a common joint point,
paired equal/opposite forces applied at the same world point. It permits rotation
about the point (no calibrated nail bending/torsional stiffness). Finite mixed-mode
force and displacement limits produce irreversible rupture. No weld/teleport.

Provisional parameters: k=12000 N/m, damping=4 N s/m, withdrawal=45 N,
lateral=70 N, displacement limit=4 mm. Reused from the earlier uncalibrated
single-joint box surrogate; these are NOT specifications of our wood or nails.
Length=75 mm selected for geometric reach across this frame, NOT calibrated
penetration or strength. Effective receiver embed scales limits up to25 mm.
Contact time=4 ms original; 2 ms tested separately as numerical sensitivity.
Do not choose strength parameters merely to make the flip succeed.

Acceptance defined before the continuous fastening+flip run:
- Four boards plus sheet meet pre-flip assembly geometry criteria.
- Physical grippers lift and turn the panel through180 degrees; no attachments.
- Hold inverted5 seconds; panel normal dot world up <-0.98.
- All4 member relative centers within5 mm and relative orientations within3 degrees
  of their assembled relation to the panel; all8 fasteners remain intact.
- All members unsupported by the fixture during the inverted hold.
- Max contact penetration <=0.5 mm; finite states/no solver warnings.
- Compare unfastened and weak-connection controls; they must not silently pass.
- Repeat a passing configuration at half timestep before treating it as numerically
  supported. A failed or interrupted trajectory is not a passed flip.

Checkpoint flip diagnostics reconstruct connection anchors from earlier recorded
shot configurations, then initialize the assembled state. Their controller uses
initial RGB and encoders, but they are explicitly PRIVILEGED INITIALIZATION /
DIAGNOSTIC evidence, not a continuous assembly or Astra-policy trial.
The --physical-nails --flip runner enables forces during the entire new assembly.
Astra must execute its own fresh trial when model access is available; no substitute
script is labeled Astra.

Required physical characterization: exact nail/shank/head geometry and embed,
wood species/grain/moisture, joint gap, quasi-static withdrawal/lateral loading,
bending/rotation response, and repeated failure loads/modes; grip pad pressure,
friction and moment capacity on the actual thin plywood. Separate calibration and
held-out specimens. Until measured, simulation failure is a model/design diagnostic,
not proof that real nails or a real gripper would fail in the same way.

2026-10-19 repair experiment (development, not held-out evaluation)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Freeze all NailParameters at the original values above. Negative controls alone
use no connections or deliberately weak 1 N limits. Keep the original 0.5 mm
penetration, 5 mm relative-position, 3 degree relative-angle and 5 s inverted-hold
criteria. Add recorded-state reporting of unintended robot/workpiece contacts
at 20 ms sampling; these must also be absent for a repaired flip to pass.

Separate interventions:
- Grasp the 76 mm frame+plywood stack at visually estimated side-member centers,
  using the unchanged native gripper, instead of carrying it by 12 mm plywood.
- Cut two 80 x 200 mm side access slots in the support footprint; retain end
  ledges supporting every member. Ledges use the original plinth's friction
  and contact compliance. This is a physical fixture design change.
- Test the original 0.68 m shoulder-center spacing separately from a 1.10 m
  spacing on the same fixed torso. The wider brackets are a hardware-layout
  change, NOT an improvement demonstrated on the original hardware. Native UR
  links, inertia, joint/actuator limits, gripper geometry and friction stay intact.
  Bracket structural adequacy is not modeled or physically validated.
- Use smooth lift/translation phases to clear torso brackets and feeder shelves
  before rotation. Nominal heights are 0.40 and 0.75 m; lateral clearance moves
  total 0.30 m. Airborne waypoint tolerances allow measured loaded-arm compliance
  within the geometry clearance budget (12 mm translation, 15 mm lift height).
  These do NOT relax the final 5 mm joint-retention or penetration criteria.
- Rotate the two wrists together at <=0.12 rad/s, gated by encoder tracking;
  retain the finite-force physical grasp throughout. No rigid attachment.

Raw diagnostic observations are retained, including failed approaches, fixture
collisions, arm-link collisions, waypoint timeouts, drops and nail ruptures.
Offline rigid-payload sweep checks write workpiece poses only in a separate
kinematic diagnostic world. They are NOT physics or policy success evidence.
The actual flip controller reads RGB-derived initial geometry and encoders;
true joint state and retention measurements remain evaluator-only. This remains
a scripted feedback skill, not a new completed Astra episode or a visual slip
observer. Runtime slip is not inferred from hidden object poses.

A successful checkpoint flip is only a mechanics development result. Require
an uninterrupted loose-parts assembly/fastening/flip run, an independent saved-
state audit, smaller-timestep check, and negative controls before describing the
repair as numerically supported. All trials on this same development layout
remain development trials, not evidence of success rates on unseen layouts.

Final numerical repair evidence
-------------------------------
Apply the 5 mm / 3 degree relative-retention limits throughout airborne motion,
not only the inverted hold. This stricter audit was recorded before run83's flip.
Runs24/25 fail this stricter transient angle check despite holding inverted.
Run83 passes continuously from loose parts at 0.5 ms. Matched runs81/84 pass at
0.5/0.25 ms; run82 fails penetration at 1 ms. Defaults now use 0.5 ms for the
clearance-stack profile. This is task-specific refinement, not contact calibration.
Panel transfer uses 18 mm carry clearance, a consistent grasp-time RGB height
reference, and planned joint-space retreat rather than a Cartesian singular path.
